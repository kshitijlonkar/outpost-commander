Mission briefing

Run a Moon base for 10 days

Every morning your solar panels make a few power cells. Walk your astronaut to the solar array, pick up a cell and carry it to a machine. A cell can only go to one machine.

  1. Life support makes oxygen. Without it the crew cannot breathe.
  2. Shield generator blocks radiation. Real solar flares from NASA records hit your base.
  3. Science lab earns points, but science does not keep anyone alive.

Keep the crew healthy for 10 days and earn at least 60 science points. Reach 100 for three stars.

Controls: arrow keys or A and D to walk, the arrow buttons on a phone, or tap a machine and the astronaut fetches a cell for it.

Day 1 of 10

What is a solar flare?

A flare is a burst of light and radiation from the Sun. Scientists rank flares A, B, C, M and X. Each letter is 10 times stronger than the one before it. Radiation can harm the crew, so the base needs a shield, like a roof of moon soil or a wall of water.

    Did you know?

    Real mission planners face the same trade-off. Power spent on science cannot protect the crew, and space weather forecasts help them decide when to work and when to take cover.

    Data and sources
    • Solar flares. Real flare dates and classes from 2024, shown as archived data. NASA tracks flares in its DONKI database (Space Weather Database Of Notifications, Knowledge, Information). When this game runs on a normal website with internet access, it asks DONKI for flares from the last 60 days and the badge at the top says Live.
    • Example events. Minor flares in archived mode and dust on the solar panels are examples, not real dates. They are labelled as examples in the game.
    • Game speed. Strong flares happen far more often in this game than in real life, so players get to practise.
    • Facts. Short facts come from public NASA information on radiation, the Moon and communications. For astronaut radiation measurements, see NASA's RadLab database linked below.
    • Links. NASA Open APIs (DONKI), Open Science Data Repository, RadLab
